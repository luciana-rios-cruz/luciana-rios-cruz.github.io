Machine Vision for Robotic Pick & Place

Academic Team Project | Tecnológico de Monterrey | March–May 2026

Vision-guided robotic manipulation using Cognex, Python, and TCP/IP communication.

PROJECT OVERVIEW

Integrated a Cognex industrial camera with an xArm robot to detect a part’s position and orientation and execute a physical pick-and-place sequence through a Python TCP server.

THE ENGINEERING CHALLENGE

Convert image measurements into robot coordinates and align the gripper with rotated parts. Key challenges included pixel-to-millimeter conversion, camera-to-robot offsets, yaw correction, and lighting sensitivity.

SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

Cognex camera: Detects the part and sends position and angle data as a TCP client.

Python server: Receives measurements, applies coordinate offsets, and generates robot commands.

xArm robot: Executes the handling sequence through the xArm SDK.

MACHINE VISION & POSITION DETECTION

Used pattern matching, circle detection, edge detection, and distance measurements to locate the part relative to a fixed conveyor sensor plate. Horizontal and vertical distances provided the part’s position.

PIXEL-TO-MILLIMETER CONVERSION

Established a reference scale of 70.172 pixels = 10 mm, equivalent to approximately 0.1425 mm per pixel. Image distances were converted into millimeters before transmission to the robot.

ORIENTATION & COORDINATE TRANSFORMATION

Calculated part orientation using atan2 from the center-to-corner displacement, with a 45° reference correction. Camera-to-robot offsets and yaw adjustments aligned the gripper with the detected part.

TCP/IP COMMUNICATION

The Python server listened on port 3000, receiving comma-separated messages containing a leading flag, X, Y, and angle. The program parsed these values and checked robot availability before executing a sequence.

PICK-AND-PLACE SEQUENCE

Detect: Locate the part and calculate its orientation.

Transmit: Send coordinates and angle to Python.

Approach: Open the gripper and move above the pickup point.

Pick: Descend, grip the part, and lift.

Place: Move to the predefined destination release the part.

TESTING & RESULTS

Validated communication, positioning above the part, and complete pick-and-place cycles with aligned and rotated parts. The robot adjusted its yaw to match the detected orientation before gripping. Tests confirmed that pixel-to-millimeter conversion and camera-to-robot offsets enabled the robot to locate, grasp, and transfer the part to a predefined destination.

MY CONTRIBUTION

I configured Cognex edge detection and distance measurements, established the pixel-to-millimeter conversion, and helped integrate camera-to-robot offsets and TCP/IP communication.

WHAT I LEARNED

Strengthened my skills in industrial vision, coordinate transformation, and robot integration. I learned how lighting, reference selection, and angular calibration affect detection reliability and gripping performance.
